GUIDE 01 / INFORMATION REQUIREMENTS

What makes an Exchange Information Requirement useful?

An EIR should tell appointed parties what information is needed, why it is needed, when it must be exchanged and how acceptance will be judged. It is not a list of software preferences or a recycled template.

Begin with decisions

Trace each requirement to a project or asset decision: design approval, cost planning, construction coordination, commissioning, maintenance or regulatory evidence. If no user or decision can be named, question whether the requirement is proportionate.

Make acceptance testable

  • Define the information container and exchange milestone.
  • State required geometry, attributes, relationships and documents.
  • Name the responsible party and reviewer.
  • Set format, status, classification and security requirements.
  • Describe the acceptance test and permitted tolerances.

A concise requirement with an objective acceptance test is more valuable than a long specification that cannot be verified.

GUIDE 02 / DIGITAL TWIN READINESS

Is your asset ready for a digital twin?

Readiness is not determined by the quality of the 3D model alone. It depends on the decision process, source data, identifiers, systems, ownership and capacity to maintain the capability.

Six questions

  • Which decision or operational outcome will the twin improve?
  • Who will use the insight, and what action will follow?
  • Which sources hold the minimum required data?
  • Are assets and locations identified consistently across systems?
  • Who owns data quality, security, changes and lifecycle cost?
  • What measurable result will determine whether a pilot should scale?

A focused pilot with one valuable decision and reliable data is stronger than a broad visualisation with no operating owner.

GUIDE 03 / COORDINATION

From clash detection to issue closure.

Automated checks can produce thousands of intersections. Effective coordination filters that noise through constructability, programme, safety, access and responsibility.

The closure chain

  • Confirm models, coordinates and versions are suitable for review.
  • Apply agreed rules and tolerances by system and zone.
  • Group duplicate clashes into decision-ready issues.
  • Assign a responsible party, priority and due date.
  • Record the agreed resolution and affected information.
  • Verify closure in the revised federated model.

The meaningful metric is not how many clashes were found. It is how many material interfaces were resolved and verified before they affected delivery.
