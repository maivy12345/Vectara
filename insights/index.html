Insight 01

ISO 19650 in plain language: it’s about controlling information, not just modelling

ISO 19650 is often mistaken for “the BIM standard about 3D models”. It is really a standard about managing information — who produces what, to what level of detail, by when, and how it is reviewed and shared. The model is one output; the discipline is the point.

Insight 02

Clash detection pays for itself before the first wall goes up

Two pipes cannot occupy the same space, but on a 2D drawing set they often appear to. Federated BIM puts every discipline’s model in one coordinate space and lets software find those conflicts automatically — hard clashes where geometry overlaps, soft clashes where clearances are violated.

Insight 03

From survey to digital twin: what reality capture actually delivers

A point cloud is millions of measured points describing what is actually on site. Reality capture — laser scanning and UAV photogrammetry — turns the physical world into data that can be compared against the design and folded into the model.

Read article →
Insight 04

Handover should be data, not a pile of PDFs

The traditional project handover is a room full of folders and a hard drive of PDFs — accurate on the day, undiscoverable a year later. A digital handover replaces it with a structured, queryable record: the verified model, the survey, and asset data the owner can actually use.

Read article →
Insight 05

Level of Information Need: more detail is not always better

A common BIM mistake is modelling everything to the highest level of detail, as if more geometry were always better. It is not. Excess detail slows the model, inflates files, and buries the information that matters under information that does not.

Read article →
Insight 06

Reality capture vs the model: comparing as-built to as-designed

The model says where things should be. Reality capture measures where they actually are. The gap between the two — as-designed versus as-built — is where quality, rework and disputes live, and a point cloud makes that gap visible.

Read article →
Insight 07

Garbage in, garbage twin: why model standards beat modelling talent

It is tempting to think a good BIM model is the work of a brilliant modeller. In practice, a model’s value comes far more from standards than from talent — from consistent naming, agreed levels of detail, and disciplined coordination than from any individual’s flair.

Read article →
Insight 08

The model is not the deliverable — the decision it enables is

BIM is sometimes sold as an end in itself, as if the 3D model were the point. It is not. The model is a means; the deliverable is a better decision made earlier — a clash resolved before construction, a quantity measured reliably, an asset handed over as operable data.

Read article →
Insight 09

The CDE is a governance tool, not a file server

The most common way to waste a Common Data Environment is to treat it as a shared drive — a place to dump files. A CDE that is just storage delivers almost none of its value, because the value is not in keeping files but in controlling their state.

Read article →
Insight 10

Why as-built data is worth more to the owner than to the builder

During construction, the model and its data serve the people building the asset. After handover, they serve the people operating it — and that is when the as-built record becomes most valuable, often to a party who was not in the room when it was created.

Insight 11

Federation order matters: why you don’t just throw models together

Federating a model sounds like combining files, but doing it carelessly produces a coordinate mess where nothing aligns and clash detection returns noise instead of insight. Federation is a disciplined act, not a drag-and-drop.

Insight 12

A model is only as current as its worst-governed contributor

A federated model feels like a single source of truth, but it is only as current as the least-disciplined discipline feeding it. One team working from a superseded model, or publishing without checking, quietly poisons the whole federation.

Read article →
Insight 13

LOD and LOIN: detail is a cost, so spend it where it pays

Two acronyms shape every BIM brief: Level of Development (LOD), how detailed the geometry is, and Level of Information Need (LOIN), what information is actually required. Confuse or ignore them and you either over-model — paying for detail nobody uses — or under-model and miss what the project needs.

Insight 14

The transmittal: boring, essential, and quietly legal

Of all the artefacts in a Common Data Environment, the transmittal is the least glamorous and among the most important. It is the record of who issued what information, to whom, in what state, and when — and that record matters far beyond housekeeping.

Insight 15

From point cloud to BIM: where the software stops and judgement begins

Reality capture produces a point cloud — millions of measured points. Turning that into a usable model is often imagined as an automated conversion. It is not. The points are data; the model is interpretation, and the interpretation is where the engineering judgement lives.

Insight 16

Coordinate systems: get the origin wrong and nothing aligns

Of all the ways a BIM effort can fail quietly, a mismatched coordinate system is among the most insidious. Each discipline’s model can be internally perfect, yet when federated they sit metres — or a whole grid — apart, and every clash result becomes noise.

Insight 17

Model handover: the formats that outlive the software

A model is only as durable as the format it is handed over in. A native file tied to a specific software version is a hostage to that software’s future; in five years the owner may not have the licence, the version, or the vendor.

Read article →
Insight 18

A clash report nobody closes is worse than no report at all

Running clash detection is easy; the value is entirely in what happens next. A clash report that lists hundreds of conflicts and is then filed unactioned is worse than no report — it creates a false sense that coordination happened while the conflicts march on to site.

Read article →
Insight 19

Naming conventions: the unglamorous backbone of a usable model

Nothing about BIM is less exciting than a naming convention, and few things matter more. When every file, model and element follows a consistent, agreed naming standard, information is findable, sortable and automatable. When it does not, the project drowns in ambiguity.

Read article →
Insight 20

Federate early, federate often

A common mistake is to develop each discipline’s model in isolation for weeks and federate only when they are ‘ready’. By then the conflicts are baked in, the rework is large, and coordination becomes damage control rather than design.

Read article →
Insight 21

The CDE access list is a security control, not an afterthought

Who can see, edit and publish information in a Common Data Environment is not an administrative detail — it is a security and quality control. An access list set up carelessly either locks people out of information they need or, worse, lets the wrong people publish into the shared record.

Read article →
Insight 22

When to trust an automated quantity — and when to check it

Extracting quantities from a model is fast and consistent, which is exactly why it can be over-trusted. The number the model produces is only as good as the model behind it; an element modelled wrong, double-counted or missing produces a confident quantity that is simply incorrect.
