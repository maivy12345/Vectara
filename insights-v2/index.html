  1. 01 Governance & CDEISO 19650 in plain language: it’s about controlling information, not just modellingISO 19650 is often mistaken for “the BIM standard about 3D models”. It is really a standard about managing information — who produces what, to what level of detail, by when, and how it is reviewed and shared. The model is one output; the discipline is the point.
  2. 02 CoordinationClash detection pays for itself before the first wall goes upTwo pipes cannot occupy the same space, but on a 2D drawing set they often appear to. Federated BIM puts every discipline’s model in one coordinate space and lets software find those conflicts automatically — hard clashes where geometry overlaps, soft clashes where clearances are violated.
  3. 03 Reality captureFrom survey to digital twin: what reality capture actually deliversA point cloud is millions of measured points describing what is actually on site. Reality capture — laser scanning and UAV photogrammetry — turns the physical world into data that can be compared against the design and folded into the model.
  4. 04 Data & handoverHandover should be data, not a pile of PDFsThe traditional project handover is a room full of folders and a hard drive of PDFs — accurate on the day, undiscoverable a year later. A digital handover replaces it with a structured, queryable record: the verified model, the survey, and asset data the owner can actually use.
  5. 05 Model standardsLevel of Information Need: more detail is not always betterA common BIM mistake is modelling everything to the highest level of detail, as if more geometry were always better. It is not. Excess detail slows the model, inflates files, and buries the information that matters under information that does not.
  6. 06 Reality captureReality capture vs the model: comparing as-built to as-designedThe model says where things should be. Reality capture measures where they actually are. The gap between the two — as-designed versus as-built — is where quality, rework and disputes live, and a point cloud makes that gap visible.
  7. 07 Model standardsGarbage in, garbage twin: why model standards beat modelling talentIt is tempting to think a good BIM model is the work of a brilliant modeller. In practice, a model’s value comes far more from standards than from talent — from consistent naming, agreed levels of detail, and disciplined coordination than from any individual’s flair.
  8. 08 Data & handoverThe model is not the deliverable — the decision it enables isBIM is sometimes sold as an end in itself, as if the 3D model were the point. It is not. The model is a means; the deliverable is a better decision made earlier — a clash resolved before construction, a quantity measured reliably, an asset handed over as operable data.
  9. 09 Governance & CDEThe CDE is a governance tool, not a file serverThe most common way to waste a Common Data Environment is to treat it as a shared drive — a place to dump files. A CDE that is just storage delivers almost none of its value, because the value is not in keeping files but in controlling their state.
  10. 10 Data & handoverWhy as-built data is worth more to the owner than to the builderDuring construction, the model and its data serve the people building the asset. After handover, they serve the people operating it — and that is when the as-built record becomes most valuable, often to a party who was not in the room when it was created.
  11. 11 CoordinationFederation order matters: why you don’t just throw models togetherFederating a model sounds like combining files, but doing it carelessly produces a coordinate mess where nothing aligns and clash detection returns noise instead of insight. Federation is a disciplined act, not a drag-and-drop.
  12. 12 Governance & CDEA model is only as current as its worst-governed contributorA federated model feels like a single source of truth, but it is only as current as the least-disciplined discipline feeding it. One team working from a superseded model, or publishing without checking, quietly poisons the whole federation.
  13. 13 Model standardsLOD and LOIN: detail is a cost, so spend it where it paysTwo acronyms shape every BIM brief: Level of Development (LOD), how detailed the geometry is, and Level of Information Need (LOIN), what information is actually required. Confuse or ignore them and you either over-model — paying for detail nobody uses — or under-model and miss what the project needs.
  14. 14 Governance & CDEThe transmittal: boring, essential, and quietly legalOf all the artefacts in a Common Data Environment, the transmittal is the least glamorous and among the most important. It is the record of who issued what information, to whom, in what state, and when — and that record matters far beyond housekeeping.
  15. 15 Reality captureFrom point cloud to BIM: where the software stops and judgement beginsReality capture produces a point cloud — millions of measured points. Turning that into a usable model is often imagined as an automated conversion. It is not. The points are data; the model is interpretation, and the interpretation is where the engineering judgement lives.
  16. 16 Model standardsCoordinate systems: get the origin wrong and nothing alignsOf all the ways a BIM effort can fail quietly, a mismatched coordinate system is among the most insidious. Each discipline’s model can be internally perfect, yet when federated they sit metres — or a whole grid — apart, and every clash result becomes noise.
  17. 17 Data & handoverModel handover: the formats that outlive the softwareA model is only as durable as the format it is handed over in. A native file tied to a specific software version is a hostage to that software’s future; in five years the owner may not have the licence, the version, or the vendor.
  18. 18 CoordinationA clash report nobody closes is worse than no report at allRunning clash detection is easy; the value is entirely in what happens next. A clash report that lists hundreds of conflicts and is then filed unactioned is worse than no report — it creates a false sense that coordination happened while the conflicts march on to site.
  19. 19 Model standardsNaming conventions: the unglamorous backbone of a usable modelNothing about BIM is less exciting than a naming convention, and few things matter more. When every file, model and element follows a consistent, agreed naming standard, information is findable, sortable and automatable. When it does not, the project drowns in ambiguity.
  20. 20 CoordinationFederate early, federate oftenA common mistake is to develop each discipline’s model in isolation for weeks and federate only when they are ‘ready’. By then the conflicts are baked in, the rework is large, and coordination becomes damage control rather than design.
  21. 21 Governance & CDEThe CDE access list is a security control, not an afterthoughtWho can see, edit and publish information in a Common Data Environment is not an administrative detail — it is a security and quality control. An access list set up carelessly either locks people out of information they need or, worse, lets the wrong people publish into the shared record.
  22. 22 Data & handoverWhen to trust an automated quantity — and when to check itExtracting quantities from a model is fast and consistent, which is exactly why it can be over-trusted. The number the model produces is only as good as the model behind it; an element modelled wrong, double-counted or missing produces a confident quantity that is simply incorrect.
